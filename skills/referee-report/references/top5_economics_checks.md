# Top-5 Economics Referee Checks

Use this checklist when the target journal or bar is Top-5, JPE, QJE, AER, Econometrica, or Review of Economic Studies.

## Contribution Bar

- State the paper's strongest contribution in one sentence.
- Ask whether the marginal contribution is conceptual, empirical design, measurement/data, quantification, mechanism, or a combination.
- Distinguish "credible and interesting" from "Top-5 level": the latter usually needs broad relevance, a clean design or model, and a result that changes how readers think about a major question.
- Do not rely on generic praise such as "important topic" or "novel data"; explain what those features buy scientifically.

## Identification Bar

- Identify the estimand and the identifying variation before listing robustness checks.
- Separate internal validity from interpretation: a clean local estimate may still have a narrow policy estimand.
- Prefer threats that would change the sign, magnitude, or interpretation of the main result.
- For RD, DiD, event-study, IV, or structural designs, ask whether the assumptions are defended in the setting rather than only by standard diagnostics.

## Measurement Bar

- Ask whether the main outcome is the object claimed or a proxy.
- If it is a proxy, state the mapping and the main ways it can fail.
- Look for decompositions that separate mechanical accounting changes from economically meaningful changes.
- For historical or archival data, check whether missingness, changing boundaries, transcription, and time-consistent units could matter.

## Mechanism Bar

- Distinguish evidence that is contemporaneous and local from evidence that is later, indirect, or from another setting.
- Ask whether the preferred mechanism is uniquely supported or merely consistent with the evidence.
- Prefer a small number of high-value mechanism tests over a long menu of weak additions.

## External Validity Bar

- Say what the setting teaches beyond itself and what remains historically specific.
- Avoid turning a local historical estimate into a broad modern policy claim without naming the bridge assumptions.

## Report-Writing Bar

- Open with an overall evaluation that tells the editor the paper's promise and the binding concerns.
- Use a few major comments, each with claim at stake, threat, why it matters, and a feasible fix.
- Keep minor comments minor. Do not bury central identification or contribution concerns in a robustness list.
- Keep anchors useful but unobtrusive. A section or table reference is usually enough; page references should not make the report read like a litigation memo.
- Strip AI-slop before delivery: remove generic throat-clearing, repetitive transition phrases, inflated praise, and over-neat scaffolding.
