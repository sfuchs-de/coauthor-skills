# Referee Report Quality Rubric

Score final drafts and evaluation outputs out of 100. A deliverable passes at 85 or above only if
all hard gates also pass.

## 1. Evidence Fidelity And Anchor Economy - 20

- 18-20: Paper-specific claims are accurate, verified, and compactly anchored; uncertainties are
  explicit; no invented facts or citation claims.
- 14-17: Mostly accurate with minor under-anchoring or clutter.
- 8-13: Material claims are vague, weakly anchored, or insufficiently verified.
- 0-7: The report invents, misstates, or cannot locate important evidence.

## 2. Contribution And Journal Calibration - 15

- 14-15: Distinguishes conceptual, empirical, measurement, and quantitative novelty, benchmarks the
  paper against its closest relevant literature in the opening assessment, and evaluates the
  contribution at the requested journal bar.
- 11-13: Credible contribution judgment with one underdeveloped comparison.
- 6-10: Describes rather than evaluates contribution.
- 0-5: Generic praise, unsupported novelty claims, or no contribution assessment.

## 3. Core Validity - 20

Use the profile appropriate to the paper:

- Empirical: literal estimand, treatment and comparison units, fixed-effect compatibility,
  variation, assumptions, threats, diagnostics, and headline-magnitude consistency.
- Theory: domains, assumptions, derivations, equilibrium closure, and claimed generality.
- Structural or mixed: empirical moments, mapping, identification, closure, economic interpretation
  and policy invariance of result-driving primitives, benchmark comparability, and counterfactual
  logic.

Award 18-20 only when the report identifies the pivotal issue and ties it to the paper's claim. For
an empirical paper, the score cannot exceed 17 if the reviewer has not reconstructed what the
headline coefficient compares or checked the central reported magnitude when the necessary inputs
are available.

## 4. Measurement, Mechanism, Or Theory-To-Evidence Mapping - 15

- 14-15: Explains what core proxies or model objects establish, what they may conflate, whether the
  observed transaction and market correspond to the modeled object, and how they support the
  mechanism or quantitative claim.
- 11-13: Strong treatment with one missing distinction.
- 6-10: Mentions the issue without analytical bite.
- 0-5: Treats measurement or mapping as self-evident or misstates it.

## 5. Structure And Prioritization - 10

- 9-10: A concise round-appropriate report with three or four coherent major comments, no
  duplicated remedies, and genuinely smaller comments.
- 7-8: Clear, but one comment is diffuse, misplaced, or repetitive.
- 4-6: Too list-like, overlong, compressed, or poorly prioritized.
- 0-3: Unstructured or not recognizable as a referee report.

## 6. Feasibility And Resolution Thresholds - 10

- 9-10: Every major comment has one proportionate primary request and a clear resolution threshold;
  optional extensions are not publication conditions.
- 7-8: One request is broad or one stopping rule is vague.
- 4-6: Several bundled or costly requests obscure what would resolve the concern.
- 0-3: Criticism has no feasible path to resolution or asks for a different paper.

## 7. Note Or Revision-Round Fidelity - 5

- 5: Notes and annotations are verified and covered; revision reports close resolved issues and
  explain any admissible new concern.
- 3-4: Coverage is mostly complete with a minor omission.
- 1-2: Notes are weakly integrated or resolved issues are reopened.
- 0: Notes are ignored or treated as facts without verification.

If no notes or prior round exist, full credit requires stating that and applying the initial-round
workflow correctly.

## 8. Tone And Prose Naturalness - 5

- 5: Diplomatic, direct, specific, and recognizably human; first-person judgment is natural rather
  than rewritten as repetitive instructions; technical language is preserved.
- 3-4: Professional but somewhat soft, repetitive, or mechanical.
- 1-2: Generic, inflated, harsh, or visibly scaffolded.
- 0: Inappropriate or unusable.

## Hard Gates

Fail regardless of score if the draft contains:

- an unsupported paper-specific fact, number, quotation, anchor, or literature comparison;
- a major comment scoring below 4/5 on fairness;
- a major comment without one primary request or a resolution threshold;
- confidential recommendation language in the author report;
- an optional extension framed as a required condition without explaining why the headline claim
  fails absent the extension;
- a revision report that silently reopens resolved concerns.
