# Deck Architecture

## Core argument spine

For a quantitative economics paper, use the following default sequence and adapt it to the actual paper:

1. **Title.** Paper title, authors and affiliations, venue/date, required disclaimer.
2. **Setting and tension.** One or two observable facts that make the economic problem concrete.
3. **Question.** The economic question and the distinction the paper introduces.
4. **This paper.** Framework, new data or measurement, quantitative exercise, and headline findings.
5. **Contribution.** Position the paper sharply against the closest literatures; use a compact literature slide only when useful for the audience.
6. **Framework or sufficient statistic.** Setup, key result, mechanism or disruption channels, and strategic interpretation.
7. **Measurement and data.** Construct the objects in the same order in which the theory introduced them.
8. **Stylized facts.** Raw patterns that discipline or motivate the quantitative model.
9. **Quantitative model or empirical design.** Explain what is added beyond the reduced-form or sufficient-statistic framework and what disciplines it.
10. **Quantification or estimation.** Inputs, variation, calibration, validation, and scenario families.
11. **Results.** Begin with the benchmark experiment, then mechanisms, heterogeneity, strategic comparisons, and policy.
12. **Conclusion.** Framework, measurement, quantitative findings, and central economic implication.
13. **Backup.** Definitions, derivations, data construction, validation, robustness, alternative scenarios, and supplementary results.

Do not force every deck to contain every item. A 20-minute conference talk should omit or compress literature, model detail, secondary facts, and robustness. A seminar may retain them and use linked backup aggressively.

## Section logic

Every section should answer one question:

- Why is the problem economically important?
- What object organizes the analysis?
- How is that object measured?
- What does the model or empirical design add?
- What do the counterfactuals or estimates show?

At a section transition, state why the previous section is insufficient and what the next section supplies. Avoid divider slides that do not correspond to a genuine argumentative transition.

## Slide jobs

Assign each substantive slide one job:

- motivation;
- setup;
- definition;
- proposition;
- measurement construction;
- stylized fact;
- model ingredient;
- identification or calibration;
- experiment;
- result;
- mechanism;
- validation;
- policy implication;
- conclusion.

When a slide performs more than one job, split it or move secondary material to backup. Do not split a tightly connected experiment and its principal result merely to satisfy a formal rule.

## Standard quantitative-results sequence

For each scenario family:

1. Define the experiment and the margin being shocked.
2. Show the primary incidence or response.
3. Explain the mechanism or decomposition.
4. Show economically meaningful heterogeneity.
5. Compare absolute and relative or strategic outcomes where relevant.
6. State support limitations and move detailed diagnostics to backup.

Use the same terminology and semantic color across scenario families.

## Timing and versions

Approximate planning benchmarks:

| Talk | Substantive slides | Typical treatment |
|---|---:|---|
| 15--20 minutes | 8--12 | Question, framework, measurement, 3--4 results, conclusion |
| 30 minutes | 15--20 | Add data construction, model summary, validation, mechanisms |
| 45--60 minutes | 22--30 | Full narrative with selected derivations and heterogeneity |

Overlays add speaking time even when they do not add numbered frames. Budget equations, maps, and tables explicitly.

Create versions in this order:

1. accepted long-deck narrative;
2. short deck derived by cuts;
3. stepped delivery version derived from the chosen talk deck;
4. speaker script matched exactly to the final slide order and reveal states.

## Backup design

Organize backup by the main argument:

1. theory and definitions;
2. data construction;
3. model or empirical implementation;
4. validation and robustness;
5. supplementary results and policy accounting.

Main slides link only to likely discussion branches. Backup slides should be self-contained enough to answer the question without returning to the paper.

## Content checks

Before completion, verify:

- every headline claim has a source in the paper, data, or model output;
- notation is consistent across theory, measurement, and quantification;
- experiment definitions precede results;
- denominators and sign conventions are explicit;
- quantitative magnitudes have an economic interpretation;
- limitations are calibrated to actual support rather than appended mechanically;
- conclusion claims do not exceed what the main results established.
