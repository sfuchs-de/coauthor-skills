# Six Additional Worked Repairs

These are original fictional examples, not quotations, findings about Barcelona,
or rewritten passages from the source papers. Each repair has a specified premise.
The source analogue concerns exposition, not the example’s economic validity.
The six final paragraphs are strict-checked under the 45-paper inventory and
screened locally for exact 12-word source overlap. These checks do not establish
scientific correctness or independent writing quality.

## E01: Timing Before A Dynamic Object

**Premise:** A firm orders capacity today; delivery occurs next period. Current
demand is uncertain, and the firm can wait instead of ordering. No result about
the direction of investment volatility is supplied.

**Weak:** A dynamic framework produces rich adjustment dynamics through the value
function, with substantial implications for the volatility of investment.

**Revision:**
> Firms choose whether to order additional capacity before they know next period's demand. An order cannot raise current output because delivery takes one period. Waiting avoids the current payment but leaves the firm with less capacity if demand rises. The firm's value therefore depends on existing capacity, outstanding orders, and demand. We use this value to compare ordering with waiting; the effect on investment volatility is a separate quantitative question.

**Map:** payment and delivery -> trade-off -> state -> value comparison -> unresolved
quantitative question. **Fidelity:** timing retained; no invented volatility result.
**Analogue:** [MY01 pp2,7](SOURCE_STUDY.md#my01).

## E02: Aggregation Before Interpretation

**Premise:** The aggregate export-to-domestic-sales ratio is a domestic-sales-weighted
mean of industry ratios. Both weights and industry ratios may change.

**Weak:** The industry effects generate an aggregate export response that captures
the broad consequences of financial development.

**Revision:**
> The aggregate ratio of exports to domestic sales is a weighted average of industry ratios, with weights equal to each industry's share of domestic sales. It can change because firms export more within an industry or because production moves toward industries that export more. We report these changes separately. A small aggregate response can coexist with large industry responses if the changes in composition offset the changes within industries.

**Map:** definition -> two margins -> reporting decision -> conditional interpretation.
**Fidelity:** no unweighted aggregation or claim about an observed offset.
**Analogue:** [L01 p18](SOURCE_STUDY.md#l01).

## E03: Fit And Validation

**Premise:** Parameters are fitted to route traffic. Trade values are not fitted.
Agreement with either object alone does not establish the proposed instrument’s
exclusion restriction.

**Weak:** The excellent model fit validates the estimates and identification.

**Revision:**
> We fit the model to route traffic and compare its predictions with two outcomes. The traffic comparison shows how closely the model reproduces its estimation target. The trade-value comparison asks whether the same parameters explain an outcome that was not used in estimation. Agreement in the second comparison supports this implication of the model, but neither comparison establishes that the instrument affects trade only through transport costs.

**Map:** fitted object -> two distinct checks -> implication -> identification limit.
**Fidelity:** no numerical fit or causal validity invented.
**Analogue:** [W02 p24](SOURCE_STUDY.md#w02).

## E04: Counterfactuals And Restrictions

**Premise:** Several payoff functions rationalize the observed choices. The researcher
has not established that the proposed policy produces the same choices or welfare
under all these functions.

**Weak:** Normalizing the model allows us to identify the counterfactual welfare effect.

**Revision:**
> The observed choices are consistent with several payoff functions. A restriction that selects one of them allows us to calculate a policy response, but does not by itself show that this response is identified. We first ask whether the other payoff functions consistent with the data imply the same change in choices. We then ask the separate question of whether they imply the same welfare change. A restriction needed for one question need not answer the other.

**Map:** equivalence -> selection versus identification -> behavior -> welfare.
**Fidelity:** no claim that either object is identified in the example.
**Analogue:** [MY04 pp2,8](SOURCE_STUDY.md#my04).

## E05: A Network Measure And A Causal Claim

**Premise:** An equilibrium model maps observed trade shares and maintained
elasticities into income exposure. The relation between that exposure and a second
outcome may involve common causes or reverse causality.

**Weak:** Theory-consistent exposure identifies how economic dependence changes policy.

**Revision:**
> The model maps trade shares and the maintained elasticities into a measure of income exposure to changes in other countries. This measure accounts for responses beyond bilateral trade. It does not establish that exposure causes a change in policy: policy may affect trade, and common shocks may affect both. The empirical analysis therefore needs a source of variation that separates changes in exposure from these other influences.

**Map:** construction -> economic coverage -> causal ambiguity -> design requirement.
**Fidelity:** model consistency retained without converting it into identification.
**Analogue:** [R12 pp2,20](SOURCE_STUDY.md#r12).

## E06: State What A Measurement Leaves Out

**Premise:** A researcher observes shipment weight and origin-to-destination distance,
but not actual routes. More stops can make actual distance longer.

**Weak:** Our comprehensive measure precisely captures global transport use.

**Revision:**
> We measure transport use by multiplying shipment weight by the distance between its origin and destination. This measure distinguishes shipping more goods from shipping them farther. It does not capture the additional distance from intermediate stops because we do not observe the full route. We therefore interpret it as a measure based on direct distance, not as an exact measure of the distance ships travel.

**Map:** observed inputs -> useful distinction -> missing segment -> interpretation.
**Fidelity:** no unjustified claim of precision or completeness.
**Analogue:** [W03 p3](SOURCE_STUDY.md#w03), [W02 p7](SOURCE_STUDY.md#w02).
