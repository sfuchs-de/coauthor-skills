# Additional Author Study

14 added papers; selected passages on 83 PDF pages. Full extraction does not imply full manual reading.

These are editorial analyses of coauthored papers, not claims of individual author style or verification of research results.

## MY01

[Time to Build and Fluctuations in Bulk Shipping](https://drive.google.com/uc?export=download&id=1OF5uW9Y6zSbXk8uGSjAAYoJcAul5dR39)

Myrto Kalouptsidi. Published American Economic Review article, February 2014.

Selected passages: PDF pp2, 3, 7, 12, 29, 40.

**Argument map:** construction delays and uncertain demand -> dynamic choices -> valuation evidence -> counterfactual competition assumption -> outcomes.

- PDF p2, sequentiality: Introduce entry and delayed operation before describing estimation.
- PDF p3, validation: Compare recovered values with separate observed prices before counterfactual analysis.
- PDF p7, mathematics: Define the firm, time, and state components before the dynamic equations.
- PDF p29, assumptions: Explain why surplus calculations require an additional assumption about competition.

## MY02

[Geography, Transportation, and Endogenous Trade Costs](https://drive.google.com/uc?export=download&id=1wWXeEs5ykuFOpzgJM7jFRzDzLPQ9QB5i)

Giulia Brancaccio; Myrto Kalouptsidi; Theodore Papageorgiou. Published Econometrica article, March 2020; includes a publisher cover page.

Selected passages: PDF pp2, 4, 9, 13, 19, 28.

**Argument map:** imbalanced trade -> carrier movements -> endogenous freight prices -> estimation -> network counterfactuals.

- PDF p4, mechanism: Describe loaded and unmatched carriers' next destinations before invoking gravity.
- PDF p9, measurement: Define the imbalance measure before interpreting regional differences.
- PDF p13, timing: Present matching, transport, waiting, and entry in their economic order.
- PDF p19, assumptions: Explain the steady-state approximation and relate it to the observed sample.

## MY03

[Search Frictions and Efficiency in Decentralized Transport Markets](https://drive.google.com/uc?export=download&id=1WO9OS0MxsrhMmQv8OAHMgxVFypcTMPee)

Giulia Brancaccio; Myrto Kalouptsidi; Theodore Papageorgiou; Nicola Rosaia. Author manuscript dated May 2023; subsequently published in QJE in 2023.

Selected passages: PDF pp2, 3, 8, 11, 19, 37.

**Argument map:** decentralized matching -> private values -> planner comparison -> parameter recovery -> policy comparison.

- PDF p3, scope: Distinguish eliminating search frictions from eliminating the platform's market power.
- PDF p8, literature: Explain how spatial continuation values change a familiar efficiency condition.
- PDF p11, mathematics: Follow value equations with descriptions of the underlying events.
- PDF p19, interpretation: Name separate derivative terms before explaining the uninternalized composition effect.

## MY04

[Identification of Counterfactuals in Dynamic Discrete Choice Models](https://drive.google.com/uc?export=download&id=13l_EVsAC9J5o3_M2jJVFh4qaEccD8NiJ)

Myrto Kalouptsidi; Paul T. Scott; Eduardo Souza-Rodrigues. Author manuscript dated February 2020; subsequently Quantitative Economics 12(2), 2021.

Selected passages: PDF pp2, 3, 6, 8, 9, 36.

**Argument map:** observational equivalence -> restrictions -> counterfactual transformation -> behavior identification -> welfare identification.

- PDF p2, identification: Begin with the object that different observationally equivalent models may disagree about.
- PDF p6, mathematics: Introduce choices, observed states, and shocks before the transition restriction.
- PDF p8, assumptions: Describe the identifying content of normalization rather than treating it as notation.
- PDF p36, interpretation: Connect the application sensitivity to dependence on payoff levels rather than differences.

## W01

[The Round Trip Effect: Endogenous Transport Costs and International Trade](https://wongwoanfoong.github.io/research/RoundTrip_Wong.pdf)

Woan Foong Wong. Published AEJ Applied Economics article, October 2022.

Selected passages: PDF pp1, 3, 5, 14, 21, 31.

**Argument map:** shared round-trip capacity -> freight-price response -> instrument -> trade response -> policy counterfactual.

- PDF p1, mechanism: State the physical joint-cost connection before the trade-policy result.
- PDF p14, facts: Use directional examples to connect freight-price asymmetry to demand asymmetry.
- PDF p21, identification: Introduce the equation, endogeneity problem, and proposed instrument in that order.
- PDF p31, comparison: Compare elasticity estimates only after discussing outcome measures and temporal aggregation.

## W02

[Entrepot: Hubs, Scale, and Trade Costs](https://wongwoanfoong.github.io/research/Entrepot_GWZ.pdf)

Sharat Ganapati; Woan Foong Wong; Oren Ziv. Published AEJ Macroeconomics article, October 2024.

Selected passages: PDF pp2, 3, 7, 11, 24, 29.

**Argument map:** shipment journeys -> indirectness and hubs -> routing trade-off -> costs -> validation -> network policy effects.

- PDF p2, measurement: Define indirect journeys before reporting how prevalent they are.
- PDF p7, scope: Explain which journey segment is unobserved and how this affects measured indirectness.
- PDF p11, transition: Use the tension between longer routes and lower costs to motivate estimation.
- PDF p24, validation: Separate targeted traffic fit from untargeted trade fit and subsequent external comparisons.

## W03

[How Far Goods Travel: Global Transport and Supply Chains from 1965-2020](https://wongwoanfoong.github.io/research/Transport_SupplyChains_GW.pdf)

Sharat Ganapati; Woan Foong Wong. Published Journal of Economic Perspectives article, Summer 2023.

Selected passages: PDF pp1, 2, 3, 10, 18, 23.

**Argument map:** historical production example -> changing supply chains -> quantity and distance -> prices and composition -> resilience.

- PDF p1, accessibility: Use a concrete production example to introduce the economic question.
- PDF p3, measurement: Explain quantity and distance components in words before sending algebra to the appendix.
- PDF p10, literature: Compare transport-cost evidence with attention to weights, values, and quality.
- PDF p18, interpretation: Return to the original product example to explain changes along the supply chain.

## W04

[Foreign and Domestic Firms: Long Run Employment Effects of Export Opportunities](https://wongwoanfoong.github.io/research/BTA_MPW.pdf)

Brian McCaig; Nina Pavcnik; Woan Foong Wong. Author manuscript dated January 2025.

Selected passages: PDF pp2, 3, 7, 14, 27, 42.

**Argument map:** export opportunity -> institutional variation -> ownership and adjustment margins -> employment decomposition -> long-run interpretation.

- PDF p2, motivation: Explain why export growth need not imply growth among domestic firms.
- PDF p14, transition: Turn conceptual predictions about adjustment margins into an empirical question.
- PDF p27, measurement: Define ownership, continuation, entry, exit, and the base year before interpreting shares.
- PDF p42, interpretation: Connect long-run employment changes to foreign entrants and exports beyond the partner market.

## L01

[Financial Development and International Trade](https://fernandoleibovici.github.io/Leibovici_JMP.pdf)

Fernando Leibovici. Author manuscript dated February 2021; subsequently JPE December 2021.

Selected passages: PDF pp2, 3, 7, 18, 39, 46.

**Argument map:** finance-intensive exporting -> credit constraints -> industry composition -> aggregate trade shares -> empirical counterparts.

- PDF p2, literature: Use prior evidence to identify a particular financing channel, not merely a topic.
- PDF p3, mechanism: Explain the decisions and industry differences before reporting aggregate implications.
- PDF p18, mathematics: Define aggregate trade shares and their industry weights before interpreting the decomposition.
- PDF p39, validation: Construct an empirical counterpart to a specific model implication.

## L02

[Financial Frictions and New Exporter Dynamics](https://fernandoleibovici.github.io/KLS.pdf)

David Kohn; Fernando Leibovici; Michal Szkup. Author-linked accepted manuscript, revised January 2015; subsequently International Economic Review, May 2016.

Selected passages: PDF pp2, 3, 6, 11, 15, 42.

**Argument map:** new-exporter dynamics -> competing frictions -> financing timing -> decision margins -> quantitative comparison.

- PDF p2, motivation: Explain which dynamic facts a successful cross-sectional model does not account for.
- PDF p3, timing: State when wages are paid and how collateral restricts finance before describing margins.
- PDF p6, literature: Treat competing explanations as potentially complementary rather than automatically excluded.
- PDF p15, scope: Distinguish changes in firm decision rules at fixed prices from general-equilibrium effects.

## L03

[Trade in Commodities and Business Cycle Volatility](https://fernandoleibovici.github.io/KLT.pdf)

David Kohn; Fernando Leibovici; Hakon Tretvoll. Author manuscript dated July 2019; subsequently AEJ Macroeconomics July 2021.

Selected passages: PDF pp2, 3, 7, 11, 19, 41.

**Argument map:** different production and trade patterns -> common price shocks -> measured real GDP -> model channels -> country evidence.

- PDF p2, mechanism: Connect differences in economic structure to heterogeneous responses to common shocks.
- PDF p11, measurement: Distinguish sectoral balances from the aggregate balance before proceeding to the model.
- PDF p19, mathematics: Specify real-GDP measurement and the empirical price index before decomposing changes.
- PDF p41, validation: Translate a comparison between country groups into a testable country-level implication.

## L04

[Quality of Life, Firm Productivity, and the Value of Amenities across Canadian Cities](https://fernandoleibovici.github.io/canada_qolprod.pdf)

David Albouy; Fernando Leibovici; Casey Warman. Author-hosted typeset Canadian Journal of Economics article, May 2013.

Selected passages: PDF pp2, 4, 6, 11, 15, 26.

**Argument map:** wage and cost differences -> amenities and productivity -> equilibrium conditions -> graphical recovery -> measurement sensitivity.

- PDF p2, definitions: Separate consumption amenities from production amenities before estimating either.
- PDF p6, mathematics: Introduce heterogeneous households and their expenditure problem before the mobility condition.
- PDF p15, geometry: Explain which distance from an equilibrium condition measures each economic object.
- PDF p26, sensitivity: Explain the direction of bias from using housing costs to approximate broader living costs.

## R11

[The Linear Algebra of Economic Geography Models](https://stephenredding.github.io/redpublish.htm)

Benny Kleinman; Ernest Liu; Stephen J. Redding. Published AEA Papers and Proceedings article, May 2024.

Selected passages: PDF pp1, 2, 3, 4, 5.

**Argument map:** spatial exposure -> preferences and mobility -> equilibrium equations -> price and wage responses -> matrix representation.

- PDF p1, motivation: Give the economic purpose of the exposure matrix before presenting its algebra.
- PDF p3, interpretation: Explain how amenity selection reconciles real-wage differences with equal expected utility.
- PDF p4, mathematics: Interpret substitution and market-size terms using the underlying expenditure and income shares.
- PDF p5, scope: Keep the elasticities and model class attached to the sufficient-statistic representation.

## R12

[International Friends and Enemies](https://stephenredding.github.io/redpublish.htm)

Benny Kleinman; Ernest Liu; Stephen J. Redding. Published AEJ Macroeconomics article, October 2024.

Selected passages: PDF pp2, 3, 6, 15, 20, 32.

**Argument map:** economic incentives -> network real-income exposure -> descriptive checks -> quasi-experimental variation -> political outcomes.

- PDF p2, identification: Separate the model-based exposure measure from the variation supporting causal claims.
- PDF p6, sequentiality: Define the economic incentive and exposure object before the network characterization.
- PDF p15, validation: Distinguish descriptive network changes from external validation.
- PDF p20, transition: Use the validation exercise to hand off to the central empirical question.
