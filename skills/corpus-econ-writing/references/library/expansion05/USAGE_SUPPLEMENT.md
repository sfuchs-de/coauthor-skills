# Additional Economic Usage

Usage distinctions are editorial aids, not vocabulary or concept approval.

| Term | Meaning | Distinction | Source analogue |
|---|---|---|---|
| time to build | Delay between an entry decision and operation. | Not the same as an adjustment cost paid immediately. | [MY01 p2](SOURCE_STUDY.md#my01) |
| value function | Expected value of an optimized dynamic choice problem. | A market-price proxy requires its own maintained mapping. | [MY01 p40](SOURCE_STUDY.md#my01) |
| endogenous trade costs | Costs respond to carrier decisions and equilibrium conditions. | Distance alone does not determine this response. | [MY02 p4](SOURCE_STUDY.md#my02) |
| steady state | Strategies depend on own state and a constant long-run aggregate state. | Observed time variation does not itself establish the approximation. | [MY02 p19](SOURCE_STUDY.md#my02) |
| composition effect | An additional customer changes the mix of match surpluses. | Distinct from matching probability and congestion effects. | [MY03 p19](SOURCE_STUDY.md#my03) |
| continuation value | Value of the future state reached after a current choice. | Not merely the current payment from the match. | [MY03 p11](SOURCE_STUDY.md#my03) |
| counterfactual identification | Admissible models agree on the specified counterfactual object. | Does not require every structural parameter to be identified. | [MY04 p2](SOURCE_STUDY.md#my04) |
| normalization | A restriction fixing otherwise undetermined payoff components. | Its implications for counterfactuals must be examined. | [MY04 p8](SOURCE_STUDY.md#my04) |
| round trip effect | Shared transport capacity connects costs in opposite directions. | Not a generic correlation between arbitrary routes. | [W01 p1](SOURCE_STUDY.md#w01) |
| trade elasticity | Response of a specified trade measure to freight prices. | Weight, value, and temporal aggregation are not interchangeable. | [W01 p31](SOURCE_STUDY.md#w01) |
| indirect trade | A shipment makes additional country stops before its final destination. | Stops and transshipment are not identical measures. | [W02 p2](SOURCE_STUDY.md#w02) |
| model fit | Agreement between predicted and observed objects. | Targeted fit and untargeted or external validation answer different questions. | [W02 p24](SOURCE_STUDY.md#w02) |
| transport use | Services required by shipment quantities and distances. | Growth in trade value alone does not measure it. | [W03 p3](SOURCE_STUDY.md#w03) |
| value-to-weight ratio | Shipment value relative to physical weight. | Composition along a supply chain can change this ratio. | [W03 p18](SOURCE_STUDY.md#w03) |
| firm adjustment status | Continuation, entry, or exit relative to a stated base year. | Separate from foreign or domestic ownership. | [W04 p27](SOURCE_STUDY.md#w04) |
| long-run employment effects | Employment changes including entry and later growth. | A bilateral opportunity can affect third-country investment. | [W04 p42](SOURCE_STUDY.md#w04) |
| aggregate trade share | Total exports relative to domestic sales of tradable goods. | Its industry weights can change alongside within-industry trade shares. | [L01 p18](SOURCE_STUDY.md#l01) |
| empirical counterpart | An observed measure or specification corresponding to a model implication. | Requires alignment of outcomes, aggregation, and conditioning variables. | [L01 p39](SOURCE_STUDY.md#l01) |
| working capital | Funds required before production revenues are realized. | Separate the payment timing from the borrowing restriction. | [L02 p3](SOURCE_STUDY.md#l02) |
| fixed-price decision rules | Firm choices compared without changing market prices. | Do not label this comparison a general-equilibrium counterfactual. | [L02 p15](SOURCE_STUDY.md#l02) |
| real GDP | Nominal value added divided by a specified GDP deflator. | Specify the index before interpreting measured productivity changes. | [L03 p19](SOURCE_STUDY.md#l03) |
| sectoral trade imbalance | Difference between exports and imports in a sector. | Aggregate balances can hide offsetting sectoral differences. | [L03 p11](SOURCE_STUDY.md#l03) |
| consumption amenities | Location attributes affecting households' utility. | An attribute may also affect production; the categories are not exclusive. | [L04 p6](SOURCE_STUDY.md#l04) |
| quality of life | The household value of location attributes under the model. | Not an arbitrary weighted ranking or an identified standalone amenity. | [L04 p15](SOURCE_STUDY.md#l04) |
| economic exposure | A model-defined response of one location to shocks elsewhere. | Expenditure, income, and response matrices have different meanings. | [R11 p4](SOURCE_STUDY.md#r11) |
| expected utility | Utility averaged over relevant idiosyncratic amenities and choices. | Equal expected utility need not imply equal observed real wages. | [R11 p3](SOURCE_STUDY.md#r11) |
| real income exposure | Economic dependence accounting for modeled network responses. | Not equivalent to the bilateral trade share. | [R12 p2](SOURCE_STUDY.md#r12) |
| quasi-experimental variation | Variation used to support identification beyond model-based measurement. | A theoretically consistent exposure does not by itself establish causality. | [R12 p2](SOURCE_STUDY.md#r12) |
