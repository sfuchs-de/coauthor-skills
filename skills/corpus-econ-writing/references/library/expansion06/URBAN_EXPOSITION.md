# Urban Exposition

## Choose The Economic Question

Use this guide with the existing argument and mathematical-exposition resources,
not as a mandatory outline for every urban paper. Choose the relevant margin:

| Question | Useful source analogue | First distinction to establish |
|---|---|---|
| Why does a city adjust slowly? | [U01](SOURCE_STUDY.md#u01) | Existing housing versus new construction; demand increases versus decreases |
| How does supply vary within a city? | [U02](SOURCE_STUDY.md#u02) | Neighborhood versus metropolitan scale; development versus intensity |
| Do higher wages imply larger gains? | [U03](SOURCE_STUDY.md#u03) | Earnings, housing costs, and conditional amenity valuation |
| Who gains from housing regulation? | [U04](SOURCE_STUDY.md#u04) | Covered tenants, owners, future renters, and supply response |
| Does infrastructure relocate activity? | [U05](SOURCE_STUDY.md#u05) | Central city versus metropolitan area; planned versus actual construction |
| What does more traffic imply? | [U06](SOURCE_STUDY.md#u06) | Travel quantity versus time, cost, and welfare |
| What can housing prices reveal? | [U07](SOURCE_STUDY.md#u07) | Capitalization, marginal buyers, heterogeneous preferences, and sorting |
| What does a neighborhood intervention identify? | [U08](SOURCE_STUDY.md#u08) | Assignment versus take-up; age versus exposure duration |

For quantitative spatial equilibrium and endogenous workplace choice, also use
the existing R01, R02, R03, R05, R06, R10, and A01 source records. The urban
extension broadens housing and empirical exposition; it does not replace that
theory coverage or endorse transferring a paper's assumptions to a new setting.

## Build The Argument

Begin with an economic contrast that requires explanation, not a list of city
characteristics. A fall in central-city population can coexist with metropolitan
growth; a higher local wage can coexist with higher housing costs. State the
geography, population, and period before explaining the contrast.

Then introduce the margin that could resolve it. Explain who chooses residence,
workplace, housing quantity, or travel, and which choices are fixed over the
studied horizon. Do not call a neighborhood a closed labor market merely because
outcomes are measured there. Distinguish residential, employment, shopping, and
ownership links when the interpretation needs them.

Let the data section supply the objects the design requires. Define parcels,
dwellings, floorspace, residents, trips, and vehicle-distance as different units.
Explain boundary vintages and crosswalks before comparing maps. A changing
average can reflect changes among incumbents or who enters and leaves; a stable
geography does not fix composition. Make the relevant population explicit.

Introduce the empirical design by explaining why the unadjusted comparison is
insufficient. For an instrument, identify the source of variation, the affected
margin, and the excluded pathways. A historical plan is not exogenous simply
because it predates the outcome. For a boundary comparison, discuss other
attributes and sorting at the boundary. For an experiment, state assignment,
take-up, and the analysis population before interpreting a coefficient.

Keep the results sequence proportional to the claim. Show the outcome directly
estimated, then the evidence for the mechanism, then any model-based incidence
or welfare calculation. Label calibrated, estimated, assigned, and residual
objects. Do not turn a fitted residual into an independently measured amenity.
Keep adverse evidence beside the conclusion it qualifies.

## Make Mathematics Earn Its Place

Before a location-choice equation, explain the household's alternatives and what
it sacrifices to obtain a location attribute. Before a housing-supply equation,
explain what quantity changes and whether land, demolition, renovation, or new
construction provides that change. Define the time horizon as well as units.

An illustrative envelope sequence can begin with expenditure
`e_n(p,r,u_n)=v_n`, with goods prices `p`, rent `r`, utility `u_n`, and resources
`v_n`. Under the stated regularity conditions and unchanged preferences, the
local money valuation is income change less the cost of initial goods and housing
purchases. Explain optimization before displaying that derivative. If amenities,
housing quality, moving costs, or feasible choices also change, introduce the
relevant terms rather than treating the initial basket formula as exhaustive.

Rent paid is an expenditure flow; house value is an asset price. A rent increase
can lower a tenant's purchasing power and raise an owner's receipts. An increase
in house value need not equal an increase in current rental income. Identify
ownership and the welfare population before aggregation; do not count a transfer
as a social gain merely by observing its recipient.

After each consequential equation, interpret a term or limiting case. Explain
what changes when supply is fixed, mobility is absent, or preferences are common.
Keep the step that connects the equation to the empirical object in the main
text. Put secondary proof pointers in footnotes without hiding indispensable
assumptions. A map or diagram should explain the same object as the equation.

## Literature And Transitions

Discuss a predecessor through the question it answers, the maintained condition
it uses, and the margin your paper adds. For example, a within-city supply study
can change the geographic scale of an earlier elasticity exercise; a child
exposure study can change the population and horizon of earlier neighborhood
evidence. Neither comparison establishes the validity of your own design.

Use the last sentence of a paragraph to state what remains unexplained. The next
paragraph should supply that missing evidence or restriction. After a descriptive
map, a move to bilateral links needs a reason: the map locates an outcome, while
the links describe how residents reach jobs or services elsewhere. After a local
estimate, a move to equilibrium needs a different reason: prices, migration, or
supply elsewhere can respond. Avoid adding a new mechanism merely to smooth a
transition.

## Review At Four Levels

| Level | Concrete check | Typical repair |
|---|---|---|
| Sentence | Do subject, outcome, unit, and population stay the same? | Name the changed object rather than using an ambiguous pronoun |
| Paragraph | Does evidence support the mechanism or only the outcome? | Separate what is measured from what the model interprets |
| Subsection | Is each new assumption needed for the next inference? | Explain its role before introducing it, and interpret the result after it |
| Section | Does the next section receive the inputs it needs? | State why descriptive facts require identification, or why local effects require equilibrium analysis |

Review figure notes for samples, weights, geographic support, price units, and
time aggregation. Review interpretation for displacement, entry and exit,
incumbent versus future residents, and tenant versus owner incidence. Do not add
all these qualifications mechanically: retain the ones consequential for the
actual claim. Read the [worked repairs](WORKED_EXAMPLES.md) for premise-preserving
examples; automated checks do not replace this review.
