# Urban Worked Repairs

Six original fictional exercises. Their premises are supplied here, not estimated
from the source papers. Source links identify exposition analogues, not support
for the fictional findings. Do not copy them into a manuscript as results.

## E01: Housing Adjustment

**Premise:** A negative local demand shock; existing housing remains usable over
the short horizon; lower values alone do not cause immediate demolition.
**Weak version:** Cheap housing demonstrates that the city is unproductive and
will lose its population immediately.

> A fall in local demand lowers the value of existing housing, but the stock does not disappear when its price falls. Over the short period we study, the same homes remain available. Lower housing costs can therefore offset part of the decline in local earnings and slow the loss of residents. This argument concerns adjustment after a negative shock; it does not imply that new construction responds in the same way to an increase in demand.

**Sequence:** shock -> durable stock -> prices -> population -> asymmetric scope.
**Fidelity:** No immediate demolition, universal response, or productivity
identification is added. Analogue: [U01 p5](SOURCE_STUDY.md#u01).

## E02: Housing Incidence

**Premise:** Rents rise; initial housing purchases are fixed for local valuation;
ownership shares and receipts must be observed to aggregate tenant and owner effects.
**Weak version:** The increase in housing prices is a loss for the city.

> Higher rents raise the cost of a tenant's initial housing purchases. They can also increase rental receipts for owners. The first effect is an expenditure change; the second is an income change. To combine them, we need to know who owns the rented homes and which residents receive the additional payments. A change in house values does not supply that information. We therefore report the tenant cost separately until ownership and rental income can be measured on the same basis.

**Sequence:** cost -> receipts -> ownership -> measurement -> bounded conclusion.
**Fidelity:** No assumption that all owners are residents, no net citywide sign,
and no equivalence between rent and house value. Analogue: [U04 p25](SOURCE_STUDY.md#u04).

## E03: Neighborhood Comparisons

**Premise:** Better test scores and higher house prices coincide across a school
boundary; household composition also differs; no causal estimate has been supplied.
**Weak version:** The price difference proves that families value better schools.

> Houses on the side of the boundary with higher test scores also sell for more. This comparison is consistent with households valuing school quality, but it does not isolate that value. Families may sort across the boundary, and other neighborhood attributes may differ with school assignment. We first examine those differences and then state the restrictions needed to interpret the price coefficient. The comparison alone is not an estimate of the effect of schools on children.

**Sequence:** observed contrast -> possible value -> sorting -> design -> causal scope.
**Fidelity:** Neither a school effect nor mean willingness to pay is manufactured.
Analogue: [U07 p31](SOURCE_STUDY.md#u07).

## E04: Infrastructure Design

**Premise:** Actual roads respond to local demand; an earlier national route plan
is proposed as an instrument, but its identifying conditions remain to be assessed.
**Weak version:** Old plans are random, so the road estimate is causal.

> Actual highway construction may respond to expected population growth. We use routes in an earlier national plan to obtain variation that does not come from the same local construction decision. The age of the plan is not sufficient for this purpose. Planned routes must predict later construction, and their relationship with the outcome must operate through the infrastructure margin under study, conditional on the included controls. We assess these conditions before interpreting the estimated population response.

**Sequence:** endogeneity -> proposed variation -> relevance -> exclusion -> inference.
**Fidelity:** No claim that a historical instrument is automatically valid.
Analogue: [U05 p8](SOURCE_STUDY.md#u05).

## E05: Travel And Welfare

**Premise:** An expansion raises vehicle-distance traveled; no travel-time or
welfare calculation has been supplied.
**Weak version:** More driving means the road expansion has no benefit.

> The expansion is followed by an increase in vehicle kilometers traveled. That outcome measures the quantity of travel, not its cost. Residents may drive more because travel becomes cheaper, because additional residents enter, or because commercial traffic grows. To evaluate congestion, we need evidence on travel time or the cost of using the road. To evaluate welfare, we also need the value of the additional trips and the cost of the expansion. The traffic estimate alone does not establish either calculation.

**Sequence:** measured quantity -> possible margins -> missing costs -> missing value.
**Fidelity:** No congestion or welfare sign is inferred from quantity alone.
Analogue: [U06 p4](SOURCE_STUDY.md#u06).

## E06: Neighborhood Treatment

**Premise:** Families are randomly offered relocation assistance; some do not
take it up; children are observed at different ages. Exclusion and monotonicity
would be additional conditions for interpreting assignment as an instrument.
**Weak version:** The experiment measures the neighborhood effect for all movers.

> Random assignment identifies the effect of being offered the program in the study population. It does not directly identify the effect of moving for every family, because some families offered assistance do not move. Using assignment to estimate the effect of take-up requires additional restrictions, including exclusion and monotonicity. We also distinguish children by age when the offer was made. Differences across ages can be consistent with longer exposure to a neighborhood, but they need not separate exposure from the costs of moving at different ages.

**Sequence:** assignment -> take-up -> added restrictions -> age -> mechanism limit.
**Fidelity:** No all-mover effect, universal age threshold, or isolated exposure
mechanism is claimed. Analogues: [U08 pp5,13](SOURCE_STUDY.md#u08).
