# Additional Author Study

8 added papers; selected passages on 55 PDF pages. Full extraction does not imply full manual reading.

These are editorial analyses of coauthored papers, not claims of individual author style or verification of research results.

## U01

[Urban Decline and Durable Housing](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w8598/w8598.pdf)

Edward L. Glaeser; Joseph Gyourko. NBER working paper 8598, November 2001; subsequently JPE 2005.

Selected passages: PDF pp3, 4, 5, 8, 12, 21, 34, 35.

**Argument map:** Persistent urban decline -> durable housing mechanism -> asymmetric adjustment -> sorting implications -> limits of the evidence.

- PDF p4, question: The introduction narrows a familiar agglomeration explanation by asking why residents remain where wages and employment are weak.
- PDF p5, mechanism: Housing durability supplies an explicit price-versus-quantity mechanism before the formal model.
- PDF p12, mathematical sequence: Geography and housing consumption are defined before the rent-distance condition; the model uses strong simplifying restrictions.
- PDF p35, interpretation: The conclusion distinguishes a predictive housing-price relationship from a causal estimate.

## U02

[The Microgeography of Housing Supply](https://drive.google.com/uc?export=download&id=1L4iUwSdUjqy1VMsvalJ6SgAWeYDkVwU2)

Nathaniel Baum-Snow; Lu Han. Author manuscript dated September 5, 2023; subsequently JPE 2024.

Selected passages: PDF pp2, 4, 6, 9, 16, 38, 39.

**Argument map:** Neighborhood policy question -> housing supply margins -> linked geography and labor demand -> local estimates -> welfare comparison.

- PDF p2, scale: The introduction makes neighborhood elasticities necessary for the policy question, rather than assuming a metropolitan estimate transfers unchanged.
- PDF p4, mechanism: The construction discussion separates parcel development from intensity conditional on development.
- PDF p16, mathematical sequence: The linked equations are accompanied by definitions of employment, residents, commuting, and the computational use of the system.
- PDF p38, interpretation: The policy comparison connects a changed geographic elasticity input to changed surplus calculations.

## U03

[The Determinants and Welfare Implications of US Workers' Diverging Location Choices by Skill: 1980-2000](https://web.stanford.edu/~diamondr/jmp_final_all_files.pdf)

Rebecca Diamond. Author manuscript dated August 27, 2015; subsequently AER 2016.

Selected passages: PDF pp2, 5, 11, 15, 29, 36.

**Argument map:** Wage inequality and sorting -> competing price and amenity interpretations -> spatial system -> identification -> group-specific welfare.

- PDF p2, question: The motivating facts permit opposing welfare interpretations; the paper states that ambiguity before choosing a model.
- PDF p11, mathematical sequence: The text distinguishes observed wages and employment from unobserved productivity before describing the estimation problem.
- PDF p29, interpretation: An amenity residual is defined through preferences and measured wages and rents, not observed directly as a named mechanism.
- PDF p5, literature: The literature discussion identifies the response margin added to earlier labor-demand analyses.

## U04

[The Effects of Rent Control Expansion on Tenants, Landlords, and Inequality: Evidence from San Francisco](https://web.stanford.edu/~diamondr/DMQOld.pdf)

Rebecca Diamond; Timothy McQuade; Franklin Qian. Author manuscript dated December 27, 2017; subsequently AER 2019.

Selected passages: PDF pp1, 5, 9, 17, 25, 30, 31.

**Argument map:** Tenant protection -> institutional assignment -> tenant and landlord responses -> dynamic valuation -> broader rental-market incidence.

- PDF p1, scope: The opening separates covered tenants, landlords, and the overall rental market.
- PDF p9, heterogeneity: Subgroups are defined using explicit age, tenure, and local rent dimensions before interpretation.
- PDF p17, mathematical sequence: The continuation-value cancellation is tied to a specified action comparison; it is not an unrestricted shortcut.
- PDF p25, interpretation: Utility components are introduced separately and converted into rent-equivalent units under structural estimates.

## U05

[Did Highways Cause Suburbanization?](https://drive.google.com/uc?export=download&id=1BTA7fyiERnQPbdeP691V_QtOKCCATJTX)

Nathaniel Baum-Snow. Author manuscript dated July 26, 2006; subsequently QJE 2007; uses highway-ray units.

Selected passages: PDF pp3, 5, 8, 14, 24, 26.

**Argument map:** Central-city decline versus metropolitan growth -> commuting mechanism -> endogenous construction -> planned-road instrument -> spatial interpretation.

- PDF p3, scale: The contrast keeps central-city and metropolitan population distinct.
- PDF p8, identification: The institutional account explains why actual construction is endogenous before introducing planned routes as instruments.
- PDF p14, estimation: The results section names the outcome, highway-ray unit, and long-difference versus panel designs.
- PDF p26, limits: The conclusion preserves the need for additional explanations of suburbanization rather than treating one estimate as a complete mechanism account.

## U06

[The Fundamental Law of Road Congestion: Evidence from US Cities](https://economics.brown.edu/sites/default/files/news/Duranton_Turner_AER_2011.pdf)

Gilles Duranton; Matthew A. Turner. Undated institution-hosted author manuscript; PDF metadata created September 13, 2010, not a verified manuscript date; subsequently AER 2011.

Selected passages: PDF pp1, 4, 11, 23, 30, 35.

**Argument map:** Road expansion and traffic -> demand-cost diagram -> endogenous capacity -> aggregate and household responses -> scope by road type.

- PDF p4, mathematical sequence: The diagram defines lane kilometers, vehicle-kilometers traveled, and average cost before formalizing their relationship.
- PDF p11, estimation: A preferred OLS specification is distinguished from the preferred estimation method.
- PDF p30, interpretation: Household travel and metropolitan traffic are different outcomes, supporting different parts of the mechanism discussion.
- PDF p35, limits: The conclusion narrows claims for road classes where instruments and boundary changes limit inference.

## U07

[A Unified Framework for Measuring Preferences for Schools and Neighborhoods](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w13236/w13236.pdf)

Patrick Bayer; Fernando Ferreira; Robert McMillan. NBER working paper 13236, July 2007; subsequently JPE 2007; main range includes the reference heading on its final page.

Selected passages: PDF pp3, 6, 12, 19, 23, 31, 32.

**Argument map:** Sorting makes preferences hard to recover -> boundary evidence -> hedonic limitations -> heterogeneous choice model -> adjusted interpretation.

- PDF p3, question: Preference recovery is motivated by allocation and sorting, not by a generic claim that neighborhoods matter.
- PDF p19, transition: The observed sorting evidence creates a reason to introduce the subsequent choice model.
- PDF p23, mathematical sequence: The second estimation step is related explicitly to the earlier price regression, so the new equation answers an existing question.
- PDF p31, limits: The conclusion explains why a boundary design can remain affected by sorting in other neighborhood attributes.

## U08

[The Effects of Exposure to Better Neighborhoods on Children: New Evidence from the Moving to Opportunity Experiment](https://opportunityinsights.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/mto_paper.pdf)

Raj Chetty; Nathaniel Hendren; Lawrence F. Katz. Author manuscript dated August 2015; subsequently AER 2016.

Selected passages: PDF pp2, 3, 5, 10, 13, 15, 27, 41.

**Argument map:** Existing neighborhood evidence -> childhood exposure hypothesis -> assignment and take-up -> defined outcomes -> age-specific estimates -> scope.

- PDF p2, literature: Earlier adult and older-youth findings motivate a different population and observation horizon, rather than being dismissed.
- PDF p13, estimation: Treatment assignment, controls, site indicators, weights, and family clustering are explained alongside the regression.
- PDF p5, limits: Age at move and exposure duration cannot be separated conclusively by this design; the mechanism discussion retains that limit.
- PDF p41, scope: The interpretation states which childhood ages are observed and which extrapolations remain open.
