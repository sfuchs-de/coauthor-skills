# Additional Source Cards

PDF page numbers below refer to the pinned downloads, not journal pagination.
These are selected readings, not full manual review of all 287 added pages.
Local extracted text supports the exact vocabulary index; the portable release
does not include source PDFs, page images, or full text.

## TH01: Milgrom And Segal

[Envelope Theorems for Arbitrary Choice Sets](https://milgrom.people.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/2002/03/Envelope-Theorems.pdf),
Econometrica 2002. Twenty-page download with a copyright cover. Selected pages
2, 4, 5, 16-18.

The value formula is separated from conditions ensuring differentiability or
absolute continuity. Pages 4-5 define the optimization problem before the
theorem and prove the local identity using inequalities. Pages 16-17 handle
parameterized constraints through the Lagrangian, not by ignoring the constraint.
Page 18 distinguishes a smooth-pasting conclusion from conditions supporting it.
Use this sequence to separate a local derivative from its regularity assumptions.

## TH02: Deaton And Muellbauer

[An Almost Ideal Demand System](https://www.princeton.edu/~deaton/downloads/An_Almost_Ideal_Demand_System.pdf),
AER June 1980, printed pages 312-326. Fifteen-page download. Selected pages 1-3, 12.

The paper begins with the modeling problem before specifying preferences. Page 2
defines expenditure at a utility level, differentiates it, and obtains budget
shares. Page 3 states restrictions and interprets the price and expenditure
responses. Page 12 discusses the static model's limitations before the appendix.
This is a useful pattern for deriving a representation before interpreting it;
the particular demand system is not a universal income-recovery theorem.

## TH03: Parry And Bento

[Revenue Recycling and the Welfare Effects of Road Pricing](https://media.rff.org/documents/RFF-DP-99-45.pdf),
RFF DP99-45, August 1999; later published in 2001. Thirty-two-page download.
Selected pages 8, 10, 11, 28.

Page 8 distinguishes time and goods constraints. Pages 10-11 interpret the
welfare terms and show the induced labor and revenue responses. Page 28 lists
partial welfare derivatives before the total policy derivative. The recycling
rule changes the economic comparison. Its labor-tax interactions require the
model's pre-existing distortion; they cannot be imported into an undistorted
budget merely by analogy. The downloaded version restricts reproduction, so
only original notes and provenance enter the release.

## TH04: Fajgelbaum And Khandelwal

[Measuring the Unequal Gains from Trade](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w20331/w20331.pdf),
NBER20331, revised August 2015; later QJE2016. Fifty-three-page download.
Selected pages 4, 6, 9, 13.

Page 4 distinguishes expenditure incidence from a richer supply-side analysis.
Page 6 groups literature by demand and expenditure questions. Page 9 derives
shares from preferences and specifies their support. Page 13 introduces the
restrictions used for its gravity representation. Read these as distinct layers
of a model, not interchangeable claims about nonhomothetic demand or identified
welfare. Baskets, support, and denominators remain consequential.

## TH05: Gandhi, Navarro, And Rivers

[On the Identification of Gross Output Production Functions](https://publish.uwo.ca/~drivers2/research/Production_Final_Full.pdf),
author manuscript August 5, 2019; JPE2020. Ninety-six pages including online
appendices. Selected pages 6, 7, 17, 18.

Pages 6-7 define information and input timing, then interpret productivity
components. Page 17 connects the flexible-input first-order condition to a share
regression and names the assumptions in the theorem. Page 18 retains the
expectation correction before integration. The progression is optimization,
restriction, recoverable object, next step. This identification argument is not
the same as a restricted-profit incidence envelope.

## TH06: Fajgelbaum And Gaubert

[Optimal Spatial Policies, Geography and Sorting](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24632/w24632.pdf),
NBER24632, revised November 2019; later QJE2020. Seventy-one-page download.
Selected pages 6, 8, 10, 13, 20.

Page 8 explains why allowing transfers changes a simple example. Page 10 keeps
aggregators general before later specialization. Page 13 defines the competitive
allocation before the planning problem. Page 20 links policy incidence to the
ownership assumption in a specialization. Use the general-to-special sequence;
do not carry a special-case cancellation into an unrestricted model.
