# Ten Worked Exposition Repairs

All examples below are original and hypothetical, not source quotations or
current BCN results. Their source analogues support an exposition operation,
not the new example's scientific assumptions. Use the operation selectively;
do not copy the wording as a template. Editorial diagnoses are not publication prose.

## 1. Introduction: A Question With An Obstacle

**Weak:** “We provide a comprehensive framework linking tourism, welfare, networks,
and policy.” The reader has not learned why these objects belong together.

**Revised:**
> A rise in tourist purchases can raise local sales without raising the income of nearby residents. Workers may live elsewhere, and residents may buy from other destinations. We use the links between workplaces, homes, and purchases to ask who receives additional income and who faces higher consumption costs.

**Why:** The question generates the need for links. It does not announce several
contributions before explaining the obstacle. Analogues: R09 p2; K05 p2.

## 2. Paragraph Flow: A Pattern Is Not A Cause

**Weak:** “The maps show tourist concentration. This reflects displacement.
The regression addresses this issue.” Each sentence silently changes the claim.

**Revised:**
> Tourist purchases and resident purchases need not rise in the same locations. Residents can receive earnings from one destination and spend them in another. Common seasonal changes can also produce this contrast. Bilateral purchase and workplace links let us measure exposure beyond the location where tourist sales occur; identifying its effect requires variation distinct from those common changes.

**Why:** Interpretation motivates the data and then the identifying problem, without
claiming the descriptive map establishes the mechanism. Analogues: R09 p17; R10 p6.

## 3. Assumption: Introduce The Operation It Permits

**Weak:** An assumption about destination substitution appears before any demand
object, followed immediately by an income formula.

**Revised:**
> Spending can rise because income increases or because purchases shift toward more attractive destinations. To separate these responses, we restrict how a destination change affects relative purchase shares across residents. Under this restriction, initial expenditure shares determine the destination adjustment to the spending response of each origin. The adjustment recovers income only within the stated demand system.

**Why:** The restriction answers a particular ambiguity. Its scope stays attached
to recovery, rather than becoming a condition for general welfare valuation.
Analogues: R04 pp3/13; R10 p22.

## 4. Producer Envelope: Explain The Vanishing Term

**Weak:** “The envelope implies price recovery.” Neither optimization nor timing is visible.

**Revised:**
> The firm chooses purchased inputs after observing its output price, while workplace labor and the input price remain fixed. At the initial optimum, an additional input purchase raises revenue by exactly its cost. Input adjustment therefore adds no separate first-order term to the change in payments available to workers and owners. The direct price effect is valued on initial output.

**Why:** The choice still adjusts. What disappears is its separate first-order
contribution under the specified optimum. Analogues: D07 pp8-10; D05 p13.

## 5. Mathematics: Definitions Before A Compact System

**Weak:** A matrix inverse is displayed first, with income shares and purchase
shares called “network weights” in the following paragraph.

**Revised:**
> Payments from a destination reach residents through work and ownership links. Residents then allocate purchases across destinations. Let the first matrix map destination payments to resident income and the second map that income to purchases. Their product describes one step of constructed feedback at baseline shares. An equilibrium response also depends on how prices and choices adjust.

**Why:** Direction and economic meaning precede matrix notation. Equal dimensions
do not establish that baseline exposure and equilibrium derivatives coincide.
Analogues: A08 p44; R08 p10.

## 6. Quantifier: Failure Of A Guarantee

**Weak:** “The spectral condition fails, so the economy has multiple equilibria.”

**Revised:**
> The sufficient condition guarantees uniqueness across the stated class of economies. Outside that region, some economies can have multiple equilibria. Its failure does not establish multiplicity for this particular geography.

**Why:** The conclusion preserves the theorem's quantifiers. A07 pp7/16 supplies
the analogue; the sentence is not a fresh proof for another system.

## 7. Results: Give A Difference Economic Content

**Weak:** “The low-income group gains more, and the results are robust.”

**Revised:**
> The lowest income group has a larger proportional gain, but the highest income group gains more in monetary units because its initial income is higher. Similar rankings under alternative recovery assumptions preserve that comparison; they do not establish that every group's gain remains positive.

**Why:** Proportional gains, monetary gains, rankings, and signs remain distinct.
Analogues: D06 pp44/56; D07 p35. No magnitudes are invented.

## 8. Literature: Compare The Inferential Operation

**Weak:** Three author summaries end with “Together these papers offer a rich framework.”

**Revised:**
> A welfare identity values changes in income and prices, whereas a demand system allows their recovery from purchases. The price index literature supplies restrictions for the second task, not evidence that spending variation is exogenous. Spatial response models address how a shock reaches other locations; their equilibrium conditions remain distinct from the identifying conditions of the regression.

**Why:** The paragraph explains different tasks and their consequences. Exact
citations must be attached to the actual source propositions, not every sentence
indiscriminately. Analogues: K05 p5; A07 pp5-6; R04 p3.

## 9. Structural Comparison: A Close Mean Is A Narrow Finding

**Weak:** “The mobile model matches the fixed model and validates our results.”

**Revised:**
> Similar aggregate gains under fixed and mobile workplaces show that this comparison changes the mean little for the specified shock. They do not imply that the same residents gain. Both models can share assumptions that the empirical income comparison does not test.

**Why:** The finding has a scope and does not acquire an unperformed incidence or
identification test. Analogues: D07 p35; R10 pp6/22.

## 10. Conclusion: Return To The Conditional Economic Answer

**Weak:** “Further work should investigate several limitations of our framework.”

**Revised:**
> Work and purchase links explain why exposure extends beyond the destinations where visitors spend. Under the maintained recovery restriction, additional income exceeds the cost of initial purchases on average. Alternative income restrictions reverse that mean while preserving much of the spatial ordering. The location of exposure is therefore clearer than the sign of the aggregate welfare gain.

**Why:** The adverse finding belongs in the answer, not a generic closing warning.
Analogues: K05 p40; D08 p41. This hypothetical paragraph does not settle the
empirical restriction for BCN or any other paper.
