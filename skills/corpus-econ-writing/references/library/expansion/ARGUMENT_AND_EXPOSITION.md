# Argument and Exposition

## What The Longer Pass Adds

The original guide covers useful paragraph operations. This supplement checks
whether those operations form a sustained economic argument. It is a selective
review method, not a required section template or a set of phrases to repeat.
Source anchors refer to the exact versions in [the source study](SOURCE_STUDY.md).
The recommendations below are editorial inferences, not universal author habits.

## 1. Give Each Part A Necessary Job

Start with the paper's question, not its current table of contents. Record what the
reader knows at the end of each section and what remains unresolved. A subsequent
section should use that knowledge to answer the unresolved question.

For an empirical welfare paper, one possible order is a measured spatial contrast,
a valuation identity, recovery of the missing responses, identification of those
responses, and interpretation of welfare. A different question can require a
different order. K05 pp2-5/15 starts from empirical disagreement before developing
the model representation; D07 pp2-5/14 carries a theoretical policy object into
estimation. These examples support dependency-based ordering, not theory-first
as a universal rule.

For each section, write a private one-sentence answer and one open question. If
two sections supply the same answer, look for repetition. If the next section
does not address a live question, look for a detour or a missing transition.
Do not put this editing exercise into the publication text.

## 2. Carry Economic Objects Across Boundaries

At sentence level, make a relationship explicit when the reader would otherwise
have to infer it. A price change becomes a change in initial purchase costs; that
cost enters welfare; welfare also requires income; income requires work and ownership
links. Connect through those objects rather than adding “therefore” to disconnected
claims. Check that the premise actually supports the consequence.

At paragraph level, use the opening to inherit an object or contrast. Give the
paragraph one main inferential task, with enough explanation to perform it. Its
ending can leave an unresolved question, interpret a result, or pass an input to
the next paragraph. It need not always summarize. A list of transitions is not a
substitute for their logical content.

At subsection level, distinguish a new task from a change in notation. Household
valuation and response recovery are different tasks even when both use spending
shares. At section level, the empirical handoff should state what theory requires
from data, not merely announce that regressions follow. A08 pp42-44 moves from
finite counterfactual shares to local comparative statics; R10 p22 makes the order
of data-based inversion explicit.

## 3. Let The Mechanism Precede The Machinery

For an important mathematical argument, establish agents, choices, timing,
constraints, and units before a compact representation. State what adjusts after
the shock and what remains fixed. A smooth general technology is not a particular
production function; workplace adjustment is not an hours response.

Give an assumption a purpose before stating it. After the assumption, explain
which operation it permits and what it rules out. Put it before the first step
that uses it, not automatically in an opening list. A proposition should refer
to its actual governing assumptions. Explain its economic meaning afterwards,
including the part that survives under weaker conditions.

D08 pp7/12-14 motivates dynamic values through the sequence of decisions. D07
pp8-10 defines choices and prices before the policy problem. These are useful
exposition analogues, not permission to import their preferences or equilibrium
closure into another paper.

## 4. Make Each Display Earn Its Space

Before a display, state why the relationship is needed. Define indices, direction
of links, domain, dimensions, units, and normalization where consequential. After
it, explain the economic terms and the next use. Do not recite every symbol again
if the preceding sentence already defined it.

For a derivation, show the step on which the argument turns. For an envelope,
explain how initial optimality eliminates a separate first-order choice term.
For an aggregate identity, identify weights and adding-up conditions. For an
inverse, separate existence of the inverse from convergence of a repeated-feedback
expansion. For a local approximation, state the expansion point and distinguish
the derivative from a finite response.

Keep matrices subordinate to economic relationships. Explain a scalar interaction,
then define matrix entries and orientation, then give the system or solution.
R08 p10 explicitly explains its change in subscript order; A08 p44 defines the
vectors and share matrices around the block system. Do not let a familiar matrix
shape obscure different income, spending, or equilibrium operators.

A07 pp6-9 distinguishes sufficiency, boundary conditions, and existence of a
counterexample outside the guaranteed region. Preserve those quantifiers.
“The sufficient condition fails” is not “this empirical economy has multiple
equilibria.” No amount of fluent prose repairs the latter inference.

## 5. Interpret Evidence Without Inventing A Mechanism

First say what differs: level, sign, rank, dispersion, timing, precision, or fit.
Give the denominator, weights, comparison group, and relevant unit. Then explain
the economic channel supported by the analysis. Mark a plausible alternative as
an alternative; do not turn a map or decomposition into identification.

A calculation can assign receipts to residences without observing changes in
their actual recipients. A normalized fixed effect can summarize several demand
components without measuring income independently. A matched baseline does not
test a model's response to a new shock. Keep these distinctions visible in the
results paragraph, not only in an appendix or a final disclaimer.

D07 p35 explains how a calibration input affects both estimation and the policy
exercise. R09 pp2/17 links a design and a mechanism while retaining alternative
explanations. R10 pp6/22 distinguishes fitting observed spatial levels from
evaluating predicted responses. Use these as checks on the inferential chain.

## 6. Discuss Literature Through The Current Question

Organize references by the object or inference at issue: valuation, response
recovery, spatial propagation, identification, or a policy exercise. Explain the
shared question or operation, the difference that matters, and its consequence.
Do not give an author list three miniature abstracts and an empty final sentence
about combining theory and data.

Separate four citation jobs: origin of an idea; precedent for a mathematical
operation; empirical comparison; and a contrasting restriction. A theorem cited
as a mathematical precedent does not validate the empirical identifying condition.
An empirical analogy at regional scale does not establish a within-city coefficient.
A comparison should reflect the actual source, not an inferred claim of priority.

K05 p5 discusses connections through its representation and labor adjustment;
A07 pp5-6 compares result domains; R08 pp4-5 distinguishes forward-looking
investment from other dynamic specifications. These show useful comparisons,
not a requirement to state novelty in every paragraph. Use footnotes for a
secondary ancestry or proof location when it interrupts the live argument.

## 7. Keep Detail, But Put It Where It Works

Fluid writing can be long. Preserve an important explanation, derivation, adverse
finding, or interpretation rather than replacing it with a compact warning. Move
long proof algebra or solver detail when the main text already supplies the
mechanism, decisive step, assumptions, and economic implication. An appendix
reference should support that argument, not replace it.

A08 p2 offers different reading paths for different tasks. R08 p5 distinguishes
main discussion, derivations, and further empirical detail. Neither establishes
a universal page limit. The useful allocation depends on what the reader needs
to evaluate the paper's claim.

## Review At Seven Levels

| Dimension | Question | Observable defect |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | Does each part answer a necessary question? | Duplicated job or missing dependency |
| Flow | Does the next sentence or paragraph inherit an object? | Unexplained jump or vague backward reference |
| Argument | Do premises support the advertised conclusion? | Valuation mistaken for identification |
| Exposition | Are choices and conditions explained before use? | Assumption or result arrives without purpose |
| Interpretation | What does this magnitude or contrast mean? | Table recital or invented causal mechanism |
| Mathematics and sequence | Are definitions, conditions, derivation, and use ordered? | Undefined symbol, transposed link, stronger quantifier |
| Literature | Does each comparison change an inference? | Author list or unsupported equivalence |

Use brief source-specific reasons for a rating. Evaluate fidelity separately:
a missing adverse result, changed assumption, or incorrect derivative fails even
when the prose reads smoothly. Word overlap and sentence lengths are descriptive
screens, not an author-similarity model. Independent evaluation is a separate
task; self-review must be labeled as such.
